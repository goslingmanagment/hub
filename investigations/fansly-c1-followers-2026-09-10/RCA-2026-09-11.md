## What the repeat means

The prior checkpoint carried generation 779 with 18,325 observed/source rows.
Generation 780 then observed 18,324 rows and matched its fresh provider headline.
The destructive predicate deliberately preserves a missing row last seen in
generation 779 until generation 781 also fails to see it. A live touch during
the sweep also protects a row. Therefore zero deactivation candidates and a
remaining active-count mismatch can occur after a correct full traversal.

This is a compatible explanation, not an identified production row. Its
identity and exact protection reason are unavailable through the restricted
reader. Live activity, reactivation after completion and roster changes between
observations remain alternatives. Equal counts at two times do not establish
that the same extra row survived. `exact_generation` proves the generation
count matches the provider headline; it does not prove all active rows match.

The repository regression now follows one missing row through both generations:
after G it remains active with active=2, generation=1 and no candidates; after
G+1 observes the same roster, only that protected row becomes inactive and the
counts agree. This tests the real database predicate, not handler certification
or executor completion. A future suppression policy also needs a test of its
actual decision path preserving this necessary second request.
