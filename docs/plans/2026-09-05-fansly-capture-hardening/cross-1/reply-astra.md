Persistent profiles are available for either supported browser. Use one profile owner, no concurrent clone and no Firefox↔Chromium profile copying. ## D2 — Incremental heads and membership: **combine**

**A head refresh can coexist with #214. My earlier objection should be narrowed to treating a partial head walk as a certified full sweep.** Keep cadence-only as the first change; add a separately tested head mode when its stopping rule and coverage contract are demonstrated.

Verified mechanics:

- `apps/runtime/src/services/sync/executor-handlers.ts:2780–2930` creates/resumes a full-scan generation and requests 100 groups/page. At `:3321` every full-scan row receives that generation; `:3337–3517` checks overlap, upserts, counts and conditionally finalizes. `:3583` requests message follow-up when needed.
- `packages/db/src/repositories/page-dm.ts:164–240` already supports the right partial-writer primitive: **passing `lastSeenGeneration: null` preserves an existing stamp**, through the monotonic conflict update; a new row receives null. Therefore a head writer need not fabricate membership.
- The current visibility update at `page-dm.ts:247` hides visible rows with a null/older generation. It has **no recent-head-touch exemption**. A head insertion during a full generation could therefore be hidden even when `count(generation) == observedCount`. A per-chunk lock alone does not resolve that race across a resumable sweep.

The concrete synthesis is two separately represented modes under the same page execution ownership:

1. **Head mode:** journal each fetched page; upsert observed rows with a null generation input and preserved message-archive state; retain partner repair and message follow-up. Never increment the full-scan count, stamp its generation, run the visibility pass or advance `lastFullSweepCompletedAt`. Keep a separate head cursor/freshness marker. Do not label an early stop a completed membership census.
2. **Full mode:** retain #214's unique generation, complete enumeration, provider-total/overlap/erasure guards and exact membership count. Only it can certify membership or hide absent rows. Start with one full scan per 24 hours, not 48, to bound the increased reconciliation lag.
3. **Initially serialize head updates outside the entire active full generation**, including gaps between its chunks. At this page size a normal 100-row full walk has roughly eleven minutes of spacing, so that is practical if measured completion agrees. If a full generation must be abandoned, withhold finalization before allowing a head refresh. Interleaving would require an explicit concurrent-insertion/touch rule in the visibility predicate and tests; monotonic stamps alone are insufficient.

`docs/decisions.md` #214 (`:6911–6985`) remains the full-membership authority. Head freshness must become a separate explicit health input; simply marking the existing full-sweep success timestamp every three hours would conceal incomplete membership. This costs a versioned cursor/policy change, mode-aware health, shared metadata-upsert code and race/rollback tests. It is substantially more than a changed `limit` and `break`.

**The proposed stopping rule is not yet a completeness proof.** Compare state by conversation ID against the pre-refresh state; “these message IDs exist somewhere” is too weak. Two unchanged pages can coexist with changed unread state, identity metadata or a changed/deleted older message farther down a mutable or pinned ordering. The adapter sends `sortOrder=1`, but the code/HAR does not establish a provider guarantee that every relevant change moves ahead of the overlap boundary. A daily full scan eventually catches some misses; it does not preserve the accepted four-hour DM target for a new head missed until tomorrow. Instrument a `would_stop` result during normal full sweeps and inspect the remaining pages for missed changes—**no duplicate platform requests**. Require no observed missed new heads over the busy-page verification window before enabling; retain reconciliation monitoring afterward. This tests the hypothesis without claiming future ordering is guaranteed.

**Request arithmetic for approximately 13,500 conversations.** `packages/fansly/src/adapter.ts:1175` declares completion on a short page, so an exactly divisible population needs a final empty request: 136 calls at limit 100, 676 at limit 20. Counts below exclude retries, detail/head-repair/message requests and native browser background traffic.

| Shape | List calls/day | Relative to observed lilly-2 ≈6,465/day |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current full scan, 100/page, every 30 min | 48 × 136 = **6,528** theoretical | 101.0%; close to, not identical to, the snapshot |
| Full scan, 100/page, every 2 h | 12 × 136 = **1,632** | **25.2%** |

Let `H` be the mean number of 20-row head pages per poll, including the two overlap pages. The daily-full design costs **8H + 136** list calls/day; a 48-hour full scan costs **8H + 68** on average. Coalescing a head poll with a full scan can reduce these conservative totals slightly. Staying below 15% of 6,465 requires approximately **H ≤104** with a daily 100-row full scan, or **H ≤112** with a 48-hour one. That is plausible, not measured. If the full scan also changes to 20/page, its daily contribution becomes 676 and the 15% condition tightens to **H ≤36**. Thus the fivefold warning applies to **full** enumeration; it does not refute an actually shallow head walk. Reject the unconditional ≤15% promise, not the architecture.

## D3 — Read execution and background activity: **combine**

Keep the browser page and its native session machinery loaded, but **do not launch with unrestricted WebSockets and background platform mutations**. I retain the offline go/no-go gate and prefer a typed call through the app's HTTP pipeline. A versioned page-origin fetch helper is an acceptable implementation only after it demonstrates the same required session/check/WAF behavior; plain `page.evaluate(fetch)` does not inherit application interceptors.

The read-side-effect concern is verified more precisely than “an idle tab marks everything read.” In permitted HAR H4 (`fansly-ui-walk-2026-08-21.har`, main app at entry 358):

- The recorded UI walk contains **14 POSTs to `/api/v1/message/ack`** with `messageIds/type` payload shape and **seven POSTs to `/api/v1/status`** with `statusId` shape. These are normal app traffic, not kernel writes.
- Around main-body character 619,704, the app distinguishes delivered, read and combined acknowledgement queues. Around 622,315 it queues delivery acknowledgements while loading unread messages. Around 631,396, incoming-message handling chooses combined acknowledgement when the active group matches and the document has focus, and delivery acknowledgement otherwise. Its acknowledgement-event handler updates message state.

Therefore **an open connected app can emit delivery acknowledgements even without deliberately opening every conversation; a focused active conversation can produce read acknowledgements**. Those are shared conversation-state changes relevant to chatters/fans. `setStatus` around 675,790 is also an explicit presence/status mutation. This does not prove that an unfocused page reads all messages, or that blocking these calls prevents every server-side activity/last-seen update caused by ordinary login/GETs.

`page.route` alone is not a complete guard. Use context-wide routing installed before page creation/navigation, no page-level override that permits extra traffic, deny new popups, and block service workers in the tested configuration. [Context routing](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-browsercontext#browser-context-route), [WebSocket routing](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-browsercontext#browser-context-route-web-socket).

I would refine my blanket “GET-only tab” wording: allow the **CORS OPTIONS preflights needed by the approved GET operations**, reviewed static assets, and narrowly defined session/login/WAF prerequisites. An OPTIONS request must not authorize a later POST mutation. Native authentication/challenge traffic has a separate owner-controlled capability; it is not permission to permit `/message/ack`, `/status`, typing, send or arbitrary telemetry. A challenge that needs an unreviewed exchange pauses capture for recovery. Transport-level page-proxy isolation remains a separate guard against direct egress; a firewall cannot distinguish encrypted GETs from POSTs inside the permitted tunnel.

If a page-origin helper is used, it needs the profile's **current active session and verified creator binding, device identity, exact pathname check behavior, app clock behavior, cookies/WAF readiness and generation invalidation**. Use `credentials: include` when reproducing the captured application's credentialed requests; let the browser supply its own transport/fetch metadata. ## D4 — 429 rule: **combine**

**End the current HTTP operation on the first 429, with no inline retry, but retain the stream as checkpoint-preserving deferred work. Publish a durable stop for both the page and its egress route.** This combines the simple request behavior with a shared deadline that survives process restarts.

Concrete initial policy, explicitly an operational choice rather than a Fansly-published limit:

1. On the first 429 in a rolling 24-hour window, set `deadline = max(valid server Retry-After deadline, DB now + 30 minutes)`. On subsequent 429s in that window use minimum delays of **60, 120, 240, then 360 minutes**, capped at 360 for our own policy; **never cap a longer valid server deadline**. A past/zero header uses the policy floor. Invalid or unrepresentable values are recorded without allowing an immediate retry; exceptionally long valid deadlines remain visible for owner review.
2. The second distinct 429 attempt within 24 hours opens one latched incident. Deduplicate by attempt identity so persistence/recovery does not increment it twice. Success between two 429s does not erase the 24-hour history. Do not change proxy/session or submit another login automatically.
3. Store page cooldown/recent-rejection state in a small page-scoped operational record, tied to the existing connection/page identity. Add a **separate `cooldown_until`** to the Fansly/global egress row in `sync_rate_limits`; preserve the existing `next_available_at` spacing field. Write both using `GREATEST(existing, new deadline)` and expose the reason/deadline through sync health. A reconnect does not automatically clear an egress cooldown.
4. Every Fansly adapter attempt checks the page and egress deadlines, reserves normal spacing, and rechecks eligibility immediately before dispatch. Reservations issued before the rejection are not exemptions. Serialize kernel dispatch per page and publish the rejection deadline before releasing that dispatch ownership. Already-started requests cannot be unsent, but they must not retry or start follow-up reads through the stop. Long waits yield a durable retry and release the worker lease.

Why a new deadline field: `packages/db/src/repositories/sync.ts:2708` reserves and advances `next_available_at` under row locks, then `apps/runtime/src/services/sync/rate-limiter.ts` sleeps outside the transaction. Advancing that timestamp after a 429 does **not** revoke earlier reservations. Why admission must be explicit: `packages/fansly/src/adapter.ts:2010` constructs its own dispatcher; putting the check only inside today's `resolveEgress().pace()` would miss Fansly. Wire the mandatory admission hook through scheduled reads, manual verification, onboarding's egress-bound verification, replay probes and the future browser bridge. DB/admission failure must fail closed. `packages/shared/src/http-client.ts:546` currently clips the server delay at 60 seconds; change Fansly's policy without silently changing other vendors.

**Extension:** fix `src/background/fansly-client.ts`'s five-second clipping and stop inline retries after 429. Record a local page/session cooldown, respecting the full valid server delay; without a usable header, use 60 seconds then 2/4/8 minutes up to 15 minutes for repeated extension failures. Return a typed deferred result to the UI, with no scheduled “try every second” loop. The kernel's much more conservative 30-minute floor is for archival traffic, not an unrequested 30-minute disablement of all live-reader features. Expose the kernel's page deadline through its scoped status contract/SDK so extension-added reads can defer during a known shared-exit cooldown. Any extension rejection report back to Hub must be authenticated and page-bound, contain no platform credentials, and remain distinct from a kernel-observed failure. This requires a small cross-repo contract change; it adds no Fansly calls.

Native chatter clicks remain outside our rate controls. Do not claim a global account/IP guarantee. These conservative backoffs can exceed normal freshness targets; display the actual throttled/degraded state rather than widening the SLA to make it green.

## D8 — Host sizing: **combine**

Use **16 GiB RAM and 4 vCPU on an isolated capture host for the 6–8-profile fleet as the initial allowance**, then right-size from measurements. This narrows my previous 16–24 GiB/4–8 vCPU range. A 300–500 MB/profile estimate may describe a warm idle case; neither plan has measured the complete Fansly process tree with the selected browser, renderer/GPU/network helpers, app state, response buffers and recovery peaks. It is not evidence that 3–4 GB total will survive real use. Hub's `apps/runtime/package.json` establishes the automation dependency; the supplied production reference supplies no relevant memory profile.

Measurement plan, with no platform load test:

1. During implementation, replay sanitized app/API fixtures on a local fake origin and start **1, 3, 6, then 8 separate persistent profiles**. Exercise cold start, steady scheduled reads, a 13,500-conversation walk, large permitted responses, browser restart and receipt redelivery. Never use real model accounts for synthetic load.
2. Measure aggregate cgroup **`memory.current`/`memory.peak`**, plus proportional process memory where available; do not sum overlapping RSS and call it unique memory. Include the controller, browser children, display server and in-memory buffers. Measure CPU, request queue delay, swap/OOM, `/dev/shm` pressure and memory growth after warm-up. Start fleet profiles gradually rather than synchronizing cold boots.
3. Initial acceptance on the 16 GiB host: over a 24–48-hour representative fixture run, browser-service p95 memory **≤10 GiB**, peak **≤12 GiB**, no OOM/swap thrashing, no sustained post-warmup growth, CPU p95 **<70% of four cores**, and no missed capture deadlines. These are engineering headroom gates, not measured results or vendor requirements. Confirm with normal ari-1 and busy-page capture during the owner-approved canary.
4. If eight profiles measure below roughly 5 GiB peak with comfortable CPU/latency headroom, an **8 GiB** host becomes a defensible cost reduction. If they exceed the 16 GiB gates, first remove unnecessary DOM/background/buffer retention; then provision **32 GiB** or additional CPU if the measured requirement remains. Do not repeatedly discard/recreate profiles to conceal a memory leak or force session churn.

This buys a bounded resource reserve without presenting it as Fansly's minimum requirement. It also keeps an unmeasured browser peak away from the existing Postgres/API host.
